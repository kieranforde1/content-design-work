Voice of the customer

Terminology inconsistency is a named, documented customer pain.

Before making the case for engineering investment, I needed customer evidence that terminology inconsistency was a real, named pain — not just a content design concern. I ran a customer feedback analysis, building a classified dataset of confirmed terminology complaints from NPS surveys, support tickets, and usability research.

270k

34k

86%

The themes are consistent across NPS, CSAT, support tickets, and usability research. Customers don't recognise HubSpot's terms, encounter the same word used for different things, and find that labels don't match actual behaviour. In high-stakes areas — AI especially — that confusion directly reduces confidence and slows adoption.

"You use the same language to discuss very different things and I'm so confused I want to scream."

CSAT survey

"In the current state of the world, you can have a lead (contact) that's a lead (lifecycle stage) that generates a lead (object)... and that's a perfectly logical sentence according to HubSpot's rules. This causes BIG headaches for customers."

Lead terminology research synthesis

"Terminology is the biggest determinant of user confidence."

Agents content study — Breeze AI Agent Builder usability research

Director-level feedback in 2024 flagged terminology problems as a visible design quality issue. Combined with the customer evidence, that gave the organisational mandate to treat this as a strategic priority rather than a maintenance task — and to present it at a company-wide design review grounded in real customer cost.

The work

Three strands, one goal.

The project runs across three interconnected workstreams. Governance addressed the documentation and human infrastructure. Building an AI-assisted prototype validated the approach. The engineering rebuild is now translating that prototype into production infrastructure.

Strand 1
Governance and workshops
Designing the governance infrastructure — a self-serve workshop toolkit for product teams to identify and document terminology gaps, piloted with three teams and scaled during Code Orange.
