Context

The most visited microapp wasn't driving signups.

HubSpot's Email Signature Generator received four times as many pageviews as the next most popular microapp. But despite the volume, it was generating very few monthly signups to HubSpot — around 468 per month.

The problem was structural: all signup CTAs appeared below the fold on the most common viewport sizes, and there was no prompt at the moment of highest intent — right after a user had successfully created and copied their signature.

The experiment

A modal at the moment of highest intent.

I led work on an experiment testing two variants. Both introduced a confirmation modal that appeared after a user copied their email signature — intercepting at the point where the task was complete and the user had the most reason to consider what came next.

The modal included a positive confirmation that the signature had been copied, and a prompt to sign up for HubSpot with a focus on how HubSpot can help with sending marketing emails.

I explored copy options based on user research and competitor review, testing different angles on value and relevance.

Before and after — the existing "Copied!" alert (left) versus the new confirmation modal with a sign-up CTA (right).
Outcomes

Monthly signups grew from 468 to 6,452.

The experiment was successful. Monthly signups from the Email Signature Generator jumped from 468 to 6,452 — a 13.8× increase. The new modal was productised and became the standard pattern for the tool.
