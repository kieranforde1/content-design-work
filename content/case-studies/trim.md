## Outcomes

Customers included New Zealand Government, English Heritage, and British Heart Foundation. Trim created a predictable subscription revenue stream for Edo — the first in the agency's history.

---

## The opportunity: turning agency expertise into a product

Edo was a digital content agency — good at its work, but exposed to the classic agency problem: revenue was project-based, feast-or-famine, and entirely dependent on winning new business. There was no recurring revenue, no product, and no way to scale beyond headcount.

I identified a gap in the market: content teams at medium and large organisations had no good way to audit their existing content at scale. They were manually reviewing pages in spreadsheets, with no connection to analytics, no way to score content quality, and no systematic prioritisation method for what to update, rewrite, or delete.

I wrote the business case, presented it to Edo's leadership, and secured the investment to build Trim. I was promoted from Senior Account Manager to Product Director to lead it.

---

## Research: 100 interviews before writing a line of code

Before Trim was built, I ran over 100 user interviews with content managers, digital editors, and heads of content at organisations ranging from charities to government departments to e-commerce businesses.

The research shaped everything: the core feature set, the pricing model, the onboarding flow, and the language we used throughout the product. I built personas and experience maps directly from the interview findings — not as documentation, but as working tools that the small development team referred to throughout the build.

<!-- IMAGE: Customer journey map from user research (slide 20) -->

**What users said they needed:** a way to see their entire content inventory in one place; integration with Google Analytics to connect page performance to content quality; some kind of scoring to make prioritisation decisions defensible to stakeholders.

**What the research revealed:** the real problem wasn't the audit itself — it was making the case internally to act on it. Users needed data they could show to a director. The product needed to produce outputs, not just surfaces.

---

## The product: a content audit tool built for non-technical users

Trim crawled a website and returned an inventory of every page — scored against readability (Flesch–Kincaid), word count benchmarks, and basic SEO signals. Integrating with Google Analytics brought pageviews, bounce rate, and time-on-page alongside each content score, so teams could see at a glance which pages were underperforming and why.

The design principle throughout was: everything should be actionable. Every metric was paired with a recommendation. Every score linked to the page it referred to. The export could be filtered and sorted so a content manager could walk into a review meeting with a prioritised action list, not a spreadsheet to argue over.

## Brand and voice: building a tone of voice from scratch

Trim needed its own brand identity, distinct from Edo. I developed the tone of voice documentation from scratch — defining the principles, writing examples, and building a short style guide the development team and any future marketing hires could work from.

The positioning was: direct, expert, and unpretentious. Content teams had been talked at by analytics vendors for years. Trim's voice assumed they already knew their domain — it just gave them better data to work with.
