It’s our source of truth for all [approved](https://app.writer.com/organization/160374/team/166809/styleguide/terms?tags=) and [disapproved](https://hubspot.styleguide.com/ux-writing-standards/terms-we-avoid) terms. # **Submitting terms**

**Anyone can submit terms to Writer\!** 

1. [Use this template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1OKTiAbuKYXROZiHVaiAED6iXry8geobDOQPYsJsjG2w/edit#gid=0) to define new terms or update existing terms.   
2. Send your terms to [\#ai-tools-writing](https://hubspot.enterprise.slack.com/archives/C03JKHSRL75) for review.  
3. Your terms will be reviewed by Content Design, Marketing, Legal, and Localization (this takes around 4 weeks).  
4. Reviewers will add any feedback to your spreadsheet, and you'll be notified once the review is complete.  
5. If your terms are approved, they’ll be added to Writer. If not, the reviewers will explain why and any relevant next steps

# **About terms**

Terms are words or phrases that meet at least one of these conditions:

* They’re unique, branded, or otherwise opinionated meanings for HubSpot (like “custom object”); or  
* They have particular usage guidelines that are easy to get wrong (like the spelling of “HubSpot”); or  
* They’re something we *don't* want people to use in the product, such as internal-only language (like “Mainsail”)

These words are all examples of **terms we want people to use:**

* “Association”: what we call a connection between objects or records in our CRM  
* “Marketing Hub Enterprise”: a paid tier for our Marketing Hub product line  
* “Permissions”: these determine which tools, features, and actions are available to a user

These words are terms that we’ve defined because **we *don’t* want people to use them:**

* “HubSpot Sales”: this is an old product name that’s now deprecated. Use "Sales Hub" instead.  
* “Enable”: this can be ableist. Use "turn on" instead.  
* “Weblog”: this is an old term that isn’t used any more. Replace with "blog".

Using terms consistently across our product helps us build better experiences by simplifying our customers’ experience. 

They’re similar to the [foundational components and patterns](https://private.hubteamqa.com/foundations-components-storybook/?path=/story/home--page) in our design system because they affect all HubSpot experiences. Terms also represent HubSpot’s brand voice, just as our design look and feel are part of our brand as well.

Approved terms are added to Writer. Writer is available to [everyone in UX, and some marketing teams](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1-3FIIZBNBx5bv4YEh0eHfpjUJcDyBuoCebpK5F5luJc/edit#heading=h.w1g4kcgbywyy) as a [web or desktop application](https://app.writer.com/organization/160374/), and [as a plugin](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1-3FIIZBNBx5bv4YEh0eHfpjUJcDyBuoCebpK5F5luJc/edit#heading=h.11w129264nxm) in tools like Figma, Google Docs, and elsewhere.

# **How to approach defining terms**

## Preliminary work

Start by asking:

* Where are customers having the most trouble, such as problems achieving their goals, completing steps, or using features?  
* Does terminology/wording contribute to that problem?  
* Are there ways to simplify the language or make it more consistent?  
* What concepts in your product — names, features, actions, objects, etc. — do customers need to know to successfully and confidently complete their tasks?  
* Are there any  commonly misused terms or internal language that shouldn’t be used in the product? Label these as “Don’t Use” terms in Writer

[Review our existing terms in Writer](https://app.writer.com/organization/160374/team/166809/styleguide/terms) to see if the term you have in mind is already included, or if there are related terms.

If you’re naming a new product or feature, remember that defining a term for the new name is just a small part of the process. [You should follow HubSpot’s product full naming process](https://private.hubteamqa.com/canvas-storybook//?path=/story/guidelines-content-product-naming--page). Then, after completing this process, you can define and submit any new terms introduced.

## Creating helpful definitions

| Do | Don’t |
| :---- | :---- |
| Write a brief summary of what the term means.  Keep it simple: just one or two short sentences. Write in a way that someone outside of HubSpot would understand.  Use plain, simple words without acronyms, or jargon Include **do** and **don’t** examples to show how terms should be used. Write out any acronyms in full (unless the acronym is more familiar than the words, and writing them out will cause more confusion). Share your draft with your colleagues so that you can align on a shared understanding of what the term means. Include your triad and Product Marketing Manager (PMM). | Define terms for every word in the product \- UI labels, navigation, onboarding content, and most other words in our product aren’t usually terms that are unique to HubSpot. Add information about when/why a term was created or updated \- just focus on simple, evergreen definitions. Define the same term multiple times to fit every single context it might appear in \- instead, aim to create a definition that’s clear and works for multiple contexts. Include rules about visual design or UI \- for example, specifying that a term should be accompanied by a tooltip. |
