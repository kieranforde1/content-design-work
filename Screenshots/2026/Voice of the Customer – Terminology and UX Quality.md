# Voice of the Customer: Terminology & UX Quality

HubSpot customers consistently say that **confusing and inconsistent terminology makes the product harder to use**, increases effort, and undermines their confidence in key workflows and features. This summary brings together customer quotes and customer‑impact metrics from surveys, support, forums, and usability research.

## 6\. AI and new features: terminology drives confidence

“However, once they get there, many users still find the language confusing, which makes them less confident.” — Breeze AI agent builder usability study ([Breeze AI Research: Simpler Findings and Next Steps](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1Z2xIwljFutmnwHE11Yro585J2QJChL-TFbQ_5qU1qgA))

“Trigger: This was the most confusing word. Many users, especially those not in technical roles, had ‘no idea’ what it meant. This is a critical problem because the trigger is what makes the agent start working.” — Breeze AI agent builder usability study ([Breeze AI Research: Simpler Findings and Next Steps](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1Z2xIwljFutmnwHE11Yro585J2QJChL-TFbQ_5qU1qgA))

“Knowledge: This word was also unclear. Some users correctly guessed it meant a database the agent could use, but many others confused it with Instructions or just didn't know what it was for.” — Breeze AI agent builder usability study ([Breeze AI Research: Simpler Findings and Next Steps](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1Z2xIwljFutmnwHE11Yro585J2QJChL-TFbQ_5qU1qgA))

“Terminology is strongly linked to user confidence – users’ understanding of the interface’s terms directly correlated with their perceived ease of use and confidence levels. Confusion around terms like ‘Trigger,’ ‘Knowledge,’ and ‘Tools’ hindered usability. Terminology is the biggest determinant of user confidence.” — Agents content study (AI agents) ([Evidence Pack – agents section](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1qgJawY1ILvVd7sPq-lkBVF9cTFj9IWexvpIj2RSWcqk))

“Users expect ‘Knowledge’ to mean something the agent uses to improve outputs… Names that are too abstract or technical reduce trust and adoption.” — Agent naming research ([Naming Knowledge and Knowledge boxes](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1mUyB2l3qyBq6pN9qasaudMhV3tSXwqYl7wPE0pIDhF0))

## 7\. Quantitative signals of customer impact

* **Terminology issues are widespread in the live product.** Automated scans of HubSpot UI strings have identified roughly **270,000 terminology‑related issues**, including about **34,000 critical issues** currently visible in product experiences. — ([Notes – Content / Terminology Checker sync](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1tOBK6yb8LP0eraI0Sk2hoETp4ksmgmh_9HcXahOx8YY), [Terminology app Button Conference pitch](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1ZAzAN4do1BXW4EtJ1RzTKVAEgQA66oGLApGO8Uv3py8), [Terminology app update – Feb 2026](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1hhLTjlkzs2ucxiAFnbccZ--oDDz-9CH5CnEDk-LiiDQ))

* **Most automated content feedback to designers is about terminology.** Around **86%** of Writer suggestions to UXers are terminology issues, and Writer surfaces roughly **1.3 million term‑based issues every week** — all tied to content customers see in product. — ([2026 CD Terminology Vision](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1JCxwCp2xIHNivpBJLZeu14EvQ7vfx3USIjNWYVn7HjU))

* **CSAT explicitly cites terminology more and more.** Terminology‑related phrases (clarity, wording, labels, terminology) are showing **increasing mentions in CSAT**, including direct complaints that HubSpot’s terminology is confusing or inconsistent. — ([Self‑Serve Product Terminology – UX Design Review](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/16d82vp2dcka93TBVVReugk1_NB4vHSADB2NXo95BoG8))

* **Terminology is tightly linked to confidence in AI.** In a Breeze AI Agent Builder study, **35% of users still reported being “Not very confident”** about setting up an agent on their own, and the research ties that low confidence directly to confusing terms like “Trigger” and “Knowledge.” — ([Breeze AI Research: Simpler Findings and Next Steps](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1Z2xIwljFutmnwHE11Yro585J2QJChL-TFbQ_5qU1qgA))

### Summary

Across surveys, support interactions, forum posts, and usability studies, customers are telling the same story:

* HubSpot’s terminology often **doesn’t match their mental models**.  
* The same or similar words are **used for different concepts**, or labels and help text **don’t match actual behavior**.  
* System messages and status labels are **unclear or misleading**.  
* In high‑value areas like AI, these terminology problems **directly reduce confidence and slow adoption**.

Fixing terminology — making labels, concepts, and messages clearer and more consistent — is therefore a direct lever for improving **ease of use, time‑to‑value, trust, and satisfaction** for HubSpot customers.
