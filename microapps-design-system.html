Context

Microapps were acquiring users, but not converting them.

HubSpot's microapps — free tools like the Email Signature Generator, Blog Ideas Generator, and Website Grader — were effective at driving traffic and lead generation. But conversion rates from microapp users into the HubSpot platform were low. Users completed the task the microapp was built for, but had no clear path into the broader product.

The company priority to grow the free and paid customer base depended on fixing this. The challenge was that each microapp was built differently, owned by different teams, and handled the transition to HubSpot in its own way — or not at all.

The work

Principles, components, and content patterns for bridge experiences.

I created a design system for "product bridges" — the moments where a microapp user is invited into HubSpot. The system covered:

  • Principles for identifying when and how to bridge (when is a bridge appropriate, and when does it get in the way?)
  • Guidance on which UI components to use and how to apply them consistently
  • A content framework for writing bridge copy — what to say, in what order, and why
  • Onboarding guidance for users after they bridge into HubSpot

The system gave teams a shared vocabulary and a consistent approach, so bridges felt like a coherent HubSpot experience rather than an ad hoc add-on.

The microapps design system document — principles, component guidance, and content patterns for product bridge experiences across HubSpot's microapp portfolio.
Outcomes

Product-bridged signups grew from 7.3% to 21.5%.

Teams across Growth Microapps and Marketing audited their microapps against the guidance. Between July 2024 — when teams started applying the system — and December 2024, the Product-Bridged Signups Rate rose from 7.3% to 21.5%.
