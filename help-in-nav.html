Context

Free and Starter users were raising support tickets instead of finding answers themselves.

In 2022, I was content design lead for an overhaul of the HubSpot help user interface for Free and Starter users. The aim was to increase the number of users finding help for themselves — reducing the volume of support incidents and getting users to answers faster.

The existing help surface was not well-designed for self-serve. It required users to navigate to long Knowledge Base articles to find answers to specific questions, and didn't surface the most relevant help content in context.

The work

Information architecture and step-by-step content snippets.

I designed the information architecture and hierarchy for help content in the new user interface. Based on existing research and previous experiments, I developed a plan to show users short, step-by-step content snippets derived from Knowledge Base articles — giving them fast, actionable answers without requiring them to read a full support article.

The IA design also included mechanisms for users to access other types of help content and, when needed, raise a support ticket. The goal was to make the right form of help easy to find in the right order of effort.

The Help in the Nav Figma documentation — information architecture for the new help UI, showing content hierarchy, step-by-step snippet patterns, and the flow between self-serve and support escalation.

Outcomes

More self-serve, fewer tickets.

The experiment led to:

  • 22.96% increase in users finding support by themselves
  • 26.65% percentage point decrease in tickets submitted by Starter customers
