Context

A fictional narrative that got in the way of the demo's job.

The HubSpot WordPress plugin demo was used to showcase features to prospective users and onboard new signups. The demo used "HubFit gym" as a fictional example business — a narrative that required every step of the demo to stay internally consistent. Any time a feature changed, or a new feature needed to be added, the entire narrative had to be adjusted to match.

Beyond the maintenance problem, the demo also had the wrong structure: it opened with less valuable features and worked up to the most important ones, which meant users who dropped off early had seen the least compelling parts.

The work

Feature-focused, ordered by value, easy to update.

I led the redesign of the demo — developing a strategy to showcase plugin features directly, without a fictional wrapper, ordered to lead with the most valuable features.

The new approach had two goals: show users the most valuable things first, and make it easy for the team to update the demo incrementally as features changed — without needing to rebuild the whole narrative. I used HubSpot's internal Copy Experiment tool to build and run the experiment.

Outcomes

12.9% more demo completions; productised as the new standard.

The experiment was successful:

  • 12.9% more demo completions
  • Users who completed the new demo were 24.3% more likely to sign up for HubSpot
  • The new design was productised and simplified the rollout of new features over the following months — achieving the maintainability goal alongside the performance one
